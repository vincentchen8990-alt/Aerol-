This educational research simulator does not issue medical instructions or control physical hardware. Steady axial-thrust idealization with explicit spin-up tangential acceleration and hub reaction; not a validated full 6-DOF spacecraft or human-adaptation simulation.

Status: —

Each forecast predicts one recorded second from the eight measured samples immediately before it, so it is a one-step comparison, not a self-running future trajectory; predictions are never fed back as inputs. A missing second is a recorded gap: nothing is interpolated and the history restarts after it. Negative forecast speeds are marked invalid and their derived g is suppressed. Derived nominal radial g = r·(2π·RPM/60)²/9.80665 with r = 0.15 m — a physics calculation, not directly measured gravity, crew exposure or a learned health prediction. Source: Otsuki et al., Nature Communications 2026, Supplementary Data 13 (MHU-2 flight log); model trained on MHU-1 (2016).

Input: eight three-axis measurements in standard gravity g, exactly 16 s apart (112 s from first to last); output: the next three-axis measurement 16 s later. Values are stored and computed in g and displayed in micro-g only by multiplying by 10⁶. What this mode is not

Not live telemetry. Not a torque-response, motor-command or inertia model: the MARS-S logs contain only time and RPM. Not an autonomous simulator: the RPM model's recursive forecasts failed evaluation and are not offered. Not a validation of the hypothetical 100 m ring or of any full spacecraft. Not a human-health or crew-exposure prediction. The hypothetical ring and its simulated-dynamics model are unchanged in the other mode.

Archived-measurement mode: published NASA/JAXA reference dimensions, recorded MARS-S RPM (2016/2017 flights, released 2026) and recorded NASA PIMS acceleration (November 2026). Models: frozen exported weights evaluated with NumPy on the server; no retraining.
