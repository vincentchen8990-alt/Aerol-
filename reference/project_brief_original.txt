# PART 1 — SCIENTIFIC OBJECTIVE

## 1.1 Research goal

Develop a computational research system that investigates personalized artificial-gravity exposure for astronauts during long-duration space missions.

Our current spacecraft design uses a rotating habitation ring to produce artificial gravity.

In conventional operation, the reference target is approximately:

\[
g_{\text{Earth}}=9.80665\ \mathrm{m/s^2}
\]

However, we want to investigate whether a personalized physiological model could eventually support adjusting that target according to an astronaut's health and gravity-exposure history.

The model should consider:

- Bone mineral density.
- Bone remodeling and skeletal-health indicators.
- Blood pressure.
- Heart rate.
- Pulse-rate variability.
- ECG-derived heart-rate variability when available.
- Respiratory rate.
- Blood oxygen saturation.
- Skin temperature.
- Electrodermal activity.
- Physical activity and exercise.
- Sleep patterns.
- Relevant nutrition information.
- Individual physiological baselines.
- Spaceflight duration.
- Cumulative artificial-gravity exposure.

The research must distinguish physiological prediction from evidence of a causal benefit from changing gravity.

Do not assume that increasing gravity necessarily improves every measured health condition.

## 1.2 Main research question

**Can multimodal physiological modeling help estimate astronaut health responses to artificial-gravity exposure, while a physics-based controller maintains a requested gravity level inside a rotating spacecraft?**

Separate the research into:

**Research question A:**

Can we accurately model Earth-based physiological measurements obtained from wearable devices?

**Research question B:**

Can we model skeletal-health and cardiovascular measurements using existing human datasets?

**Research question C:**

Does available artificial-gravity research support estimating physiological responses to different exposure levels?

**Research question D:**

Can a spacecraft controller safely and accurately reproduce a requested artificial-gravity target under changing engine thrust and mechanical conditions?

Treat these as distinct experiments.

## Required physics

Steady rotational acceleration:

\[
a_r=\omega^2R
\]

For a steady axial thrust component:

\[
g_{\mathrm{effective}}
=
\sqrt{
a_{\mathrm{engine}}^2+
(\omega^2R)^2
}
\]

Calculate the steady target RPM analytically when physically feasible.

Model finite motor torque:

\[
I\dot{\omega}=\tau_{\mathrm{net}}
\]

Extend the dynamics to include the reaction of the hub to changes in ring angular momentum.

\[
a_t=R\dot{\omega}

\[
g_{\mathrm{effective}}
=
\sqrt{
a_{\mathrm{engine}}^2+
(R\omega^2)^2+
(R\dot{\omega})^2
}
\]

Implement the underlying acceleration vectors rather than calculating magnitudes alone.

- Spin-down.
